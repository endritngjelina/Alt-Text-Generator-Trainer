Alt Text Best Practices

Be Concise

Keep descriptions under 125 characters when possible. Focus on the essential information.

Be Specific

Avoid vague terms like "image" or "picture". Describe what's actually in the image.

Context Matters

Consider the image's purpose. A product photo needs different details than a decorative image.

Avoid Redundancy

Don't start with "image of" or "picture of". Screen readers already announce it's an image.

Examples: Good vs. Bad

BAD

"Image of a dog"

GOOD

"Golden retriever puppy playing with a red ball on grass"

BAD

"Picture of food on a table"

GOOD

"Caesar salad with grilled chicken, parmesan, and croutons"

BAD

"A person working"

GOOD

"Woman typing on laptop at a modern office desk with plants"

Key Principles

  • Prioritize content: Describe what matters most for understanding the image's purpose
  • Use plain language: Avoid jargon unless it's necessary and your audience understands it
  • Consider emotion: For artistic or emotional content, describe the mood or feeling
  • Include text: If the image contains important text, include it in the alt text
  • Decorative images: Use empty alt text (alt="") for purely decorative images
